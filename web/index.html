CIVIC delivers instant, authoritative plain-language legal guidance for Indian citizens under the latest legislative frameworks including the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) 2023, Motor Vehicles Act 1988 (as amended 2019), and Constitutional Rights.

Current Indian Statutory Frameworks Covered

  • Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS): Modern procedural safeguards, arrest protocols, search and seizure rules, and digital evidence memos replacing CrPC.
  • Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS): Codified offenses, citizen protections against wrongful restraint (Sec 126), and procedural violations replacing the Indian Penal Code (IPC).
  • Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 & Central Motor Vehicles Rules: Traffic checkpoint rules, digital document acceptance via Digilocker/mParivahan, and impoundment limitations.
  • Constitution of India: Fundamental Rights including Article 14 (Equality), Article 19(1)(a) (Freedom of Speech & Recording in Public), Article 20(3) (Right against Self-Incrimination), Article 21 (Right to Life & Personal Liberty), and Article 22 (Protection against Arbitrary Arrest).

Key Citizen Rights Scenarios

Traffic Stop & Vehicle Key Seizure

Traffic police cannot arbitrarily remove vehicle ignition keys or deflate tires. Under Sections 130 and 206 of the Motor Vehicles Act, an officer can ask for documentation inspection. Confiscating keys is strictly unlawful without suspicion of an active cognizable crime.

Recording Police Officers in Public

Under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution of India, citizens have the recognized right to video-record public officials performing public duties in public areas. Police cannot seize mobile phones or compel citizens to delete footage.

D.K. Basu Mandatory Arrest Guidelines

Law enforcement officers must wear visible identification tags with name and designation, prepare an arrest memo countersigned by an independent witness, allow notification to a friend or relative within 8 to 12 hours, and ensure regular medical checkups.

Arrest of Women Protocol

Under Section 43 BNSS 2023 (formerly Section 46(4) CrPC), women cannot be arrested after sunset and before sunrise except with prior written order from a Judicial Magistrate. Women must only be arrested by female police officers.
